# Statistical Mechanics --- Lecture Notes

## Purpose

This repository contains study notes prepared to accompany selected lectures in a YouTube statistical mechanics lecture series. The notes are intended as a convenient reference for revision: they organize the main ideas, definitions, equations, and reasoning into a lecture-notes format.

## Sources and credit

The notes are based on the following original YouTube lectures:
[Course | Statistical Mechanics by Leonard Susskind at Stanford](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLpGHT1n4-mAsJ123W3fjPzvlDHOvIhHA0)


**Credit for note preparation:** ChatGPT (OpenAI) has been used to generate these notes from the linked lecture and the transcript provided. The notes have been updated here as generated. The notes may contain omissions or errors; compare them with the original source when accuracy matters.

    --- introductory notes on microstates and macrostates, multiplicity,
    probability, entropy, and equilibrium. These are topic-based study
    notes and have not been verified against a full transcript.
-   [`statistical_mechanics_lecture_2_transcript_based_notes.md`](statistical_mechanics_lecture_2_transcript_based_notes.md)

    statistical entropy, equilibrium distributions, and the direction of
    heat transfer.
-   [`statistical_mechanics_lecture_3_notes.md`](statistical_mechanics_lecture_3_notes.md)
    --- notes on the entropy of a probability distribution, the laws of

    Lagrange multipliers. These are based on a caption-based transcript

    the video line by line.
-   [`statistical_mechanics_lecture_4_notes.md`](statistical_mechanics_lecture_4_notes.md)
    --- notes on the Boltzmann distribution from entropy maximization,
    the partition function, the identification of $$\beta$$ with inverse
    temperature, and the ideal gas (with and without gravity). These are
    based on a caption-based transcript obtained from a third-party page
    and have not been checked against the video line by line.

## Formatting

Mathematical expressions in these Markdown files follow GitBook's syntax: `$$...$$` within a line of text for inline mathematics, and `$$` on its own line before and after the formula for display mathematics. Single-dollar `$...$` is not used, because GitBook does not render it.

-   Check equations and interpretations against the original lecture.
-   Treat the review questions as prompts for self-testing.

    explanation.
