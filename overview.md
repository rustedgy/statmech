# Statistical Mechanics — Overview

This repository collects notes prepared to accompany selected lectures in the Leonard Susskind statistical mechanics course.

## Core ideas

The central problem of statistical mechanics is to explain macroscopic behavior from microscopic possibilities.

- A microstate is a very detailed description of the system.
- A macrostate is a description in terms of broad observables such as energy, volume, or temperature.
- The multiplicity of a macrostate is the number of microstates compatible with it.
- Entropy is closely related to multiplicity by the Boltzmann relation: $S = k_B \ln \Omega$.

## Why this matters

A system can have many possible microscopic arrangements, but only some of those arrangements correspond to typical macroscopic behavior. Statistical mechanics explains why the macroscopic world looks stable and predictable even though the microscopic world is noisy and fluctuating.
