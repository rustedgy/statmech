# Statistical Mechanics — Lecture 2

## Statistical reasoning about large systems

Statistical mechanics studies systems with many microscopic degrees of freedom. Tracking each particle individually is often impractical, so the focus shifts to probabilities and multiplicities.

The key distinction is between:

- microstate
- macrostate
- multiplicity

For large systems, most configurations are concentrated near the macroscopic values associated with the largest number of compatible microstates.

## Probability from counting

If all relevant microstates are equally likely, then:

$$
P(A)=\frac{\Omega_A}{\Omega_{\mathrm{total}}}.
$$

This allows macroscopic behavior to be inferred from counting microscopic possibilities.

## Entropy as multiplicity

Boltzmann entropy is the logarithm of multiplicity:

$$
S = k_B \ln \Omega.
$$

This gives a precise statistical measure of the number of microscopic arrangements compatible with a macroscopic description.

## Equilibrium and constraints

Equilibrium is associated with the macrostate or range of macrostates that dominate the accessible possibilities under the relevant constraints. This is not a claim that microscopic motion stops; it is a statement about which macroscopic states dominate the probability distribution.

## Key takeaways

- entropy measures multiplicity
- equilibrium corresponds to dominant accessible states
- large systems concentrate around typical macroscopic behavior
